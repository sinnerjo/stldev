Coaching Licenses Worth Getting as a Volunteer: What Each One Actually Covers

By Joseph Warren · Youth soccer coach and creator of SubGo · October 11, 2026 · 8 min read

You do not need every credential a coaching education page can throw at you. Some of what follows is not really optional at all, some of it is genuinely worth the time and money, and some of it is built for people chasing coaching as a career rather than a volunteer running one team. Here is what each one actually covers, what it costs, and where the honest line is.

Who this guide is for

A recreational or club volunteer coach trying to figure out which certifications matter for the team in front of you, not a coach already on a professional pathway.

Not optionalWhat comes before any license at all

Three things are not really "coaching education" in the usual sense, they are the baseline your club almost certainly requires before you are allowed near a team, license or no license.

  • SafeSport Trained. The U.S. Center for SafeSport's own site states the individual cost directly: "the cost of an individual 90 minute SafeSport Trained Core course is $20 per person." If you are registered through a soccer organization recognized by the U.S. Olympic and Paralympic Committee, which includes US Soccer, you likely will not pay it yourself, since "individuals under the jurisdiction of a National Governing Body recognized by the U.S. Olympic and Paralympic Committee are not subject to a fee." The certificate is only good for a year: "SafeSport Trained certificates are valid for 12 months after the completion date." This is not a club preference either. It traces back to a real federal law, the Protecting Young Victims from Sexual Abuse and Safe Sport Authorization Act of 2017, which requires organizations like US Soccer to provide this training to adults in regular contact with minor athletes.
  • A background check. US Soccer's Safe Soccer Clearance Program bundles this in: "the background check element of the program costs $24 and lasts for two years."
  • Concussion awareness training. The CDC offers a free course built for coaches specifically, "HEADS UP to Youth Sports Coaches: Online Concussion Training," aimed at helping coaches "spot signs and symptoms of possible concussions and steps to take if one occurs." Whether it is legally mandatory depends on your state and league rather than one national rule, but plenty of state soccer associations require it outright. Mississippi Soccer Association's own policy is typical of how this gets enforced: "all adult participants including coaches, assistant coaches, team managers, club staff, and board members must complete the CDC HEADS UP Concussion Training" before taking part in any sanctioned activity, alongside SafeSport training and a background check, all tied to US Soccer's Safe Soccer Framework. Check your own state association's specific policy rather than assuming Mississippi's applies to you, since this is set state by state.

None of the three above are things to skip because they feel like paperwork. They expire on a schedule, SafeSport yearly, the background check every two years, so budget a little recurring time for them every season, not just once.

The free starting pointIntroduction to Grassroots Coaching

Before any format specific course, US Soccer requires a short introductory module, free and about 20 minutes according to the state associations that administer it. It is the prerequisite for everything else on this list that runs through US Soccer, and it is worth doing in your first week as a coach regardless of what level you end up coaching.

Worth itThe 4v4, 7v7, 9v9, and 11v11 Grassroots courses

These are the courses that actually teach you something format specific, each a four hour course covering, in the Missouri Youth Soccer Association's own words, "Grassroots Coaching Education Philosophy, Play Practice Play Methodology, the Six Tasks of a Coach." Worth knowing before you sign up for all four: not every one counts toward every license path. Missouri's own association is direct about this: "only the 7v7, 9v9, and 11v11 courses are accepted for the MYSA Coaching License requirement. The 4v4 License is not accepted, as 4v4 play is not used at the Competitive level." If you coach nothing but the youngest recreational ages, the 4v4 course still teaches useful content, it just may not be the one your state counts toward a license requirement.

Pricing here is set by your state association or club, not by US Soccer nationally, and it genuinely varies, roughly $75 to $125 depending on where you looked in recent research. Check your own state association's page for the current number rather than trusting a figure from another state.

A real exampleWhat level actually requires what

How much of this you actually need depends heavily on what you coach, and state associations differ on exactly where the line sits. Wisconsin Youth Soccer Association publishes a clean, tiered example worth using as a mental model even if you coach elsewhere: "recreational coaches are required to have, at minimum, completed 1 In Person Grassroots Module," coaches of "12U to 14U Competitive and State League teams" need "2 In Person Grassroots Modules, one must be 9v9 or 11v11," and coaches of "15U and older State League teams" are required to have "the US Soccer D License." That is a real, published example of how one state actually scales the requirement to the level of team you coach, not a rule that applies everywhere. Check your own state association's specific tiers, since Missouri's own published guidance focuses on which courses count toward its license rather than laying out identical age based tiers.

The real next stepThe US Soccer D License

This is the license worth genuinely considering if you are coaching a competitive or travel team for more than one season, or if your state ties it to coaching older competitive age groups the way Wisconsin does above. It is a real time commitment. Missouri Youth Soccer Association describes it as "a 40 to 45 hour course, held over two weekends, at minimum 4 weeks apart," while Florida Youth Soccer's own page lists it as "32 hours, through a minimum of 9 weeks," a small discrepancy between states worth expecting rather than being surprised by. Prerequisites typically include the introductory module plus, per Missouri, "a minimum of three grassroots courses, in the form of two in person courses and one online course," one of which must be the 11v11 course.

Cost is where this gets serious, and again it is state administered rather than a single national number, ranging from around $400 to over $600 depending on the state association hosting it in recent research. Missouri's own association page does not publish a dollar figure, so contact MYSA directly for current pricing rather than assuming another state's cost applies here.

Probably not, and here is whyThe C, B, and A Licenses

These exist, and no official source states outright that a volunteer should skip them, but the structure of each one makes the intended audience pretty clear. The C License, one real state example shows, costs "$1,650" and runs "four months, including summative assessment, with 4 in person days," and you need to already "hold a US Soccer D license for at least six months" plus "access to a team playing 11v11, U13 and up, for the duration of the course." The B License goes further still, requiring the C License held for a year and three or more years of coaching experience, aimed at "U13+ or senior performance environments." Four figures, months of commitment, and a required team to coach through it, that is a real career investment, not something to pick up because a rec team asked you to help out this fall. If you are coaching one team as a volunteer, the D License is very likely the ceiling worth reaching for, not the floor to a longer pathway.

A different pathUnited Soccer Coaches diplomas

United Soccer Coaches, formerly known as the NSCAA, runs a separate track from the US Soccer license system entirely, and the organization is direct about the difference in its own FAQ: "our courses are called diploma programs and are intended to be information oriented," while "US Soccer... offers coaching certification courses that lead to coaching licenses." A diploma is not a substitute for a license, it is a different kind of credential built around information rather than certification.

The diplomas are organized by age group in a way that maps neatly onto the same formats above: the "4v4 Diploma, designed for coaches with players ages 8 and under, can now be enjoyed and completed exclusively online," the "7v7/9v9 Diploma, for the next level of coaching, focuses on the 9 to 12 year old player," and the "11v11/Pre-National Diploma is designed for an intermediate coach" working with the 13 and up age group. Our goalkeeper basics guide actually draws on this same organization's separate Goalkeeping Level I Diploma, which is a good example of how these diplomas fill in specific gaps a general license course will not cover. Full membership runs $150 per year according to the organization's own join page, though a cheaper Recreation Coach tier appears to exist without a published price, worth a direct question to the organization if that tier fits you better.

The actual verdictWhat is worth it, by situation

  • Every volunteer, no exceptions. SafeSport Trained, a background check, and whatever concussion training your state or club requires. These are not a choice.
  • Coaching only the youngest recreational ages. The free intro module, plus the 4v4 course if you want the content, even knowing it may not count toward a formal license in your state.
  • Coaching 7v7 through competitive club ages. The format specific Grassroots courses for your age group are genuinely useful and inexpensive, worth doing even beyond whatever the minimum requirement is.
  • Coaching a competitive or travel team for multiple seasons. The D License is the real, worthwhile investment once you know you are staying in it.
  • Considering the C License or beyond. Know exactly what you are signing up for first, four figures, months of time, and a team you can coach through it, before you commit.
