How to Train AI on Your Coaching Philosophy First

By Joseph Warren · Youth soccer coach and creator of SubGo · October 15, 2026 · 7 min read

Before you ask AI for a single practice plan or parent email, spend ten minutes building something more useful than any one request: a short, reusable profile of who you are as a coach. Your age group, your format, your priorities, your tone. Answer a guided set of questions once, save the result, and every future request starts from your actual situation instead of a generic one. This guide walks through building that profile, the exact starting prompt to hand the AI, and the real product features that make it stick across future conversations.

Who this guide is for

Anyone who has used AI for a one off practice plan or email and gotten something a little too generic back. This is the setup step before our own detailed guide to using AI as a coach, and it is worth doing first.

Why botherA generic answer is a context problem, not an AI problem

Ask a general purpose AI chatbot for a soccer practice plan with no other information and it has to guess: what age, what format, how long, what you already worked on, whether you care more about winning Saturday or building skill over a season. It will guess reasonably and hand you something plausible and generic. Ask the same question after it already knows you coach a U10 7v7 team, that you prioritize equal playing time and individual ball skill over results, and that your field time is short, and the answer changes from plausible to actually useful, without you typing any of that out again.

This is not a trick, it is how these tools are actually designed to work. Anthropic's own developer documentation on prompting is direct about it: "setting a role in the system prompt focuses Claude's behavior and tone for your use case. Even a single sentence makes a difference," and more generally, "providing context or motivation behind your instructions... can help Claude better understand your goals and deliver more targeted responses." A coaching profile is exactly that kind of context, written once and reused, instead of a single sentence buried in one request and forgotten by the next.

Step oneWhat actually goes into a coaching profile

Answer these in plain language, a paragraph or a few bullet points is plenty. There is no required format, only real specifics instead of vague ones.

  • The basics. Age group, format, roster size, practice days and length, and how much of a season you have left.
  • Your priorities, in order. Development versus results, and where equal playing time sits for you. Our equal playing time guide is a useful reference point if you have not put this into words before.
  • Your actual coaching philosophy. Anything you believe strongly, dribbling before passing, small sided games over lines, a specific formation you are building toward. Our guides on developing dribblers first, practice as a game, not a drill, and formations by age are examples of the kind of belief worth stating up front, instead of explaining it over again every time.
  • Your communication style. Direct and brief, warm and detailed, formal or casual with parents. Whatever tone you actually want your drafted emails to sound like.
  • Real constraints. Field access, equipment, weather patterns, a multilingual roster, anything that changes what advice is actually usable for your team.
  • What you will not paste in. No full names, no photos, no addresses, no birthdates. State this in the profile itself, the same standing rule our detailed AI guide covers, so it travels with every future request automatically instead of being something you have to remember each time.

Step twoThe actual prompt to hand the AI

You do not have to write the profile from a blank page yourself. Ask the AI to interview you for it. This is the literal prompt to start with:

"I coach youth soccer and want to build a short, reusable profile of my coaching philosophy and team so I do not have to explain myself all over again every time I ask you something. Interview me one question at a time about my age group, format, roster size, season length, my coaching priorities and philosophy, and my communication style. When you have enough, write a short profile summarizing my answers in plain language, written so I can paste it at the start of future conversations. Do not include any player names, photos, or personal details in the profile, only team level information." Paste this into a new conversation to start

Answer its questions honestly and specifically. A finished profile usually reads something like this, roughly a paragraph:

"I coach a U10 girls 7v7 recreational team, twelve players, two practices a week at an hour each, with about eight games left this season. My top priority is equal playing time and individual ball skill, ahead of winning. I believe in developing dribblers first and letting passing emerge later as the field grows, and I run practice as small sided games rather than lines and drills. I want practice plans built around a 4-2-1 shape we are working toward. My communication style with parents is warm but brief, and I always want specific, checkable sources for any rule or statistic before I repeat it to a family. Do not include any player names, photos, or personal details in anything you draft for me."

Save that result somewhere you can find it again, a notes app, a document, a saved message, whatever you already use.

Step threeMaking it actually persistent, not just saved

A saved paragraph only helps if it actually reaches every future conversation. There are three real ways to do that, from simplest to most automatic.

Paste it manually, every time. Works with any AI tool, no account feature required. Keep the profile somewhere you can copy from in a few seconds, and paste it at the top of a new conversation before your actual question. This is the most reliable method because nothing is hidden. You always know exactly what context the AI has.

ChatGPT's Custom Instructions. OpenAI's own help documentation describes the feature plainly: "custom instructions allow you to share anything you'd like ChatGPT to consider in its response," and once saved, "your custom instructions are applied immediately to all chats," existing and new. Paste your coaching profile in here once and it applies automatically going forward, without retyping it.

Claude's Projects. Anthropic's support documentation describes Projects as places that "allow you to create self-contained workspaces with their own chat histories and knowledge bases," where "you can define project instructions for each project to further tailor Claude's responses." Create a project for your team, paste your coaching profile in as the project's instructions, and every conversation started inside that project already knows who you are.

Both major AI products also now offer a separate, automatic memory feature that picks up context from your conversations over time without you writing anything, OpenAI's own documentation describes it noticing "relevant preferences and details from your chats," and Anthropic's describes Claude remembering things like "your role, projects, and professional context" and "communication preferences and working style" by default. That is a real, useful complement to a written profile, but it builds up gradually and is less precise than a profile you wrote and reviewed yourself. Do the manual version first. Let automatic memory add to it, not replace it.

Step fourTreat it as a living document, not a one time setup

Revisit the profile when something real changes: a new season, a format change from 7v7 to 9v9, a shift in priorities after tryouts, a philosophy you have refined since the season started. A profile from last August describing a team that has since changed rosters, formats, or goals will quietly steer every future answer in the wrong direction, confidently and without any warning that it is doing so. Set a reminder to reread and update it at the start of every season, the same way you would revisit a preseason parent meeting checklist.

Watch outCommon mistakes

  • Writing it once and never updating it, so a season old profile quietly shapes answers that no longer fit your actual team.
  • Making it too vague to matter, "I coach kids and want them to have fun" gives the AI almost nothing more specific than no profile at all. Real numbers and real stated priorities are what make this work.
  • Putting a player's name, photo, or personal detail into a persistent profile, which is worse than a one off mention in a single chat, since a persistent profile resurfaces in every future conversation by design.
  • Assuming a saved profile in one AI tool carries over to a different one. Custom Instructions, Projects, and a manually pasted profile are each specific to the product you set them up in.
  • Skipping the interview prompt and writing the profile solo when you are not sure what to include. Letting the AI ask the questions usually surfaces details you would not have thought to write down unprompted.
